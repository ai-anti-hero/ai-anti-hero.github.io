### The Core Concept

At its heart, a Large Language Model (LLM) is a machine learning model trained
to recognize patterns in language and predict what comes next. Rather than
retrieving answers from a database of pre-written responses, it learns
statistical patterns and relationships between words, phrases, and concepts from
large datasets of text.

When you provide a prompt, the model generates a response by predicting one
token at a time, using the preceding context to determine what comes next. A
token can represent a whole word, part of a word, or a punctuation mark. The
process continues until the response is complete or another stopping condition
is reached.

**Training (Pre-training):** Developers train the model using enormous datasets
that may include books, articles, websites, and code repositories. During
training, the model adjusts billions or even trillions of internal numerical
values, called parameters, to improve its ability to recognize patterns and
predict tokens.

<AntiHero title="The Anti-Hero" icon="information">
Once an LLM is trained and deployed, its underlying parameters are frozen and
the model itself is **stateless**—each request is an isolated pass where tokens
go in and predicted tokens come out. No matter how much you interact with,
correct, or instruct the model during a conversation, those core weights do not
update or permanently learn from your session; any continuity comes from the
application re-sending your prior chat history inside the prompt on every turn.
</AntiHero>
