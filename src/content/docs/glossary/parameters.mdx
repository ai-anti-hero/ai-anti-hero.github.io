**Parameters** are the millions or billions of internal numerical values a
model learns during training and uses to process input and predict what comes
next.

### The Basics

Think of parameters like millions of tiny volume knobs or dimmer switches on a
giant soundboard. No single knob stores a specific fact or pre-written answer;
instead, billions of settings work together to recognize patterns and generate
language. Once a model finishes training and is deployed for everyday use, those
knobs are locked in place—chatting with an AI passes your prompt through its
existing settings rather than rewriting them.

### More Detail

Mathematically, parameters consist primarily of **weights** and **biases**
arranged in large matrices across neural network layers. During pre-training and
fine-tuning, optimization algorithms iteratively adjust these floating-point
values to minimize prediction error across massive datasets. At inference time,
incoming tokens are converted into vectors and multiplied through those frozen
weight matrices to compute probability distributions over the next token.

Parameter count directly governs memory footprint and compute cost. In standard
**dense** models, every parameter activates for every token, so a 70B model
requires roughly ten times the compute and memory of a 7B model. Newer
**Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)** architectures cut per-token compute by routing each
token through only a subset of active parameters.

<AntiHero title="The Anti-Hero: Chats Don't Train the Model" icon="information">
During normal use, a deployed model's parameters are locked in. Nothing you type
in an ordinary chat rewrites its weights—making the model itself stateless. It
doesn't permanently learn from your conversations. When an AI "remembers"
something from earlier in your thread, it’s just re-reading your chat history as
context to guess the next token, not updating its internal settings. Changing
actual parameters takes a separate training or fine-tuning run.
</AntiHero>
