**Tokens** are the fundamental units of text that large language models
process and generate. A token can represent a whole word, part of a word,
punctuation mark, whitespace sequence, or even a single character or byte.
Rather than processing text directly as humans read it, language models convert
text into numbered tokens and use mathematical operations to predict what comes
next.

## The Basics

Models don't read words; they do math. Before an AI processes your prompt, a
tokenizer maps your text into smaller pieces and assigns each piece a unique
number called a token ID. Common words like "apple" usually get a single ID,
while longer words like "unbelievable" get split into smaller chunks — each with
its own ID.

Do not mistake "Token ID" with "Token". The token is the piece of text or byte
sequence. Token ID is the integer index pointing to the specific token in the
vocabulary table.

In English, 1,000 tokens equals roughly 750 words, though that ratio
drops fast depending on the tokenizer. Programming code and non-English
languages require considerably more tokens to say the exact same thing.

## More Detail

Tokenizers use algorithms to construct vocabularies during model development.
These vocabularies typically contain tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands
of entries.

**Tokenizer training (pre-LLM):** A tokenizer is typically trained or selected
before the main LLM weights are pretrained. Its vocabulary and encoding rules
are established before the model learns its token embeddings and language
representations.

**Vocabulary lock:** Once established, the tokenizer's vocabulary,
token-to-ID mapping, and encoding rules are normally kept fixed throughout
pre-training, fine-tuning, and inference. Changing them requires corresponding
changes to the model's embedding and potentially output layers to preserve
compatibility with the learned weights..

The resulting vocabulary and tokenization rules are saved alongside the model.

**Runtime tokenization:** At runtime, the tokenizer converts text into token IDs
using its predefined vocabulary and encoding rules. Each token ID indexes a
learned numerical vector called an embedding. These vectors provide the model's
initial representations of tokens, which are subsequently transformed through
the neural network to capture contextual, syntactic, and semantic information.
