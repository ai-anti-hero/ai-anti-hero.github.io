## What is an Anti-Hero?

In storytelling, an anti-hero lacks traditional heroic qualities—idealism, 
polish, blind optimism—yet still delivers the heroic outcome: solving real 
problems and protecting people from wasted time and money.

## Why an AI Anti-Hero is Needed Now

The AI conversation is dominated by two loud extremes:
1. **The Evangelists:** Selling an elixir, promising AGI tomorrow, and 
   claiming every workflow should be handed over to autonomous agents.
2. **The Doomers:** Convinced the machines are waking up and coming for 
   everyone's livelihood.

The **AI Anti-Hero** sits in the back row—building things that work, 
measuring real results, and treating AI like the tool it actually is. We 
don't claim to see the future. Instead, we look at what these systems can 
reliably do *today* and make measured, pragmatic decisions about where they 
belong. Right now, that role is a power tool, not a person.

<RealTalk title="The Guy in the Back">
When a developer claims they've become 1000% more productive, the AI Anti-Hero 
raises an eyebrow. AI might help you write a function 1000% faster, but 
raw typing speed doesn't equal shipped, reliable product delivery.
</RealTalk>

## Who's Writing This

I'm not an AI researcher, a venture capitalist, or a full-time content creator. 
I'm a working engineer who has spent the last two decades keeping real-world 
infrastructure, test systems, and CI pipelines from catching fire—and 
building local, self-hosted AI workflows to solve actual problems.

My goal is to pry open this black box called AI and understand how it actually 
behaves. They say nothing tests your grasp of a skill like teaching it. Can 
anyone truly "master" a moving target like AI? I have my doubts. But there is 
always room to get better at using the tool—and remembering that's all it is: 
a tool, not a person.
