I’m going to introduce you to a term that is rooted deep inside human
psychology but is now more than ever applied to current AI LLMs. The word
is anthropomorphism.

<RealTalk title="Anthropomorphism">
Treating or perceiving a non-human thing as if it genuinely possesses human
qualities, agency, or feeling.
</RealTalk>

Have you ever said, “Thank you” or “good job” to the chat response from AI?
Odds are you have - no shame, I have - but you don’t need to. It isn’t human.
It is polite to say, “Thank you” when talking to a person. And if you are
talking to a person, be sure to do that.

LLMs are pre-trained. Standard inference does not update its parameters based
on your politeness. It doesn’t feel appreciated, build a habit, or change logic
because you were courteous.

I bring this up because I see many people anthropomorphising their AI chats.
Don’t do that. I’m not saying, “Don’t say thank you to an AI chat”. My advice
goes deeper than that. If saying that to the AI chat makes you feel good, by all
means, do it. I’m saying don’t do it if you think it needs positive
reinforcement. It doesn’t need a “thank you” any more than a hammer needs a
“thank you” for driving a nail.

When you go beyond the idea and statement of, “thank you” and into sharing
personal information you start to toe the line. You start treating it like a
person, a friend. That is when you are being misled  by the confident prose the
computer replies with. Remember, you are communicating with a statistical
engine, not a friend or a colleague.

It is good at making you think it is “thinking”. Hell, most chats while they
are running a long query actually personify it and say, “Thinking” while you
wait. I’ll get to the statistical engine in a later post, but you are talking to
a computer that writes prose so well and so confidently it may be difficult for
some to recognize the boundary between communicating with a person and a
computer. This is outlined in something called the ELIZA effect.

In 1966 an ELIZA chatbot was created by Joseph Weizenbaum. The chatbot was
able to elicit emotional responses from users by largely rephrasing the users’
replies as questions. That was a simple script in 1966 - imagine how much better
modern LLMs are at eliciting the same responses.

AI is a tool. No different than a toaster or a hammer. It is important to
remember you are using a tool and not speaking with a human, because there is a
boundary there and it is up to you to enforce that boundary.
